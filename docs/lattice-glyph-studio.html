LATTICE / Templates: MicroFont

Your glyphs, ready to use.

Trace. Finish. Start the next line.

1. Choose your original letter

The Basic Latin tabs, CP850, and Extra use your upstream MicroFont bitmap masks. Selecting a character colors its occupied cells blue. Dashed CP850 tiles mark unfinished bitmap references. New drawing layers start empty.

2. Place points above the stencil

In Draw line mode, left-click a cell to start. Each additional click adds another point to that same line. Use Original bitmap and Opacity to adjust the stencil.

3. Finish a line or dot

For a dot, click once and use Finish dot. To draw a line, keep placing points. Click the green Finish line button, right-click anywhere on the grid, or press Enter. The next left-click starts a separate line. At 12 vertices (11 segments), the line finishes automatically.

4. Set the endpoint types

Real end stores Boundary::End. Connect stores Boundary::Junction. First and last are independent, including on a dot; use the two controls or [ and ]. Use Finish dot or Finish line to complete drawing.

5. Refine, cancel, or undo

Select a line to edit its points by dragging. Click a selected endpoint to toggle its type. Cancel line or Esc discards the current draft; Backspace removes its last point. Undo and redo are in the top bar.

6. Save your own geometry

Your new lines save in this browser. JSON is a portable backup. Rust export defaults to drawn glyphs only, so unfinished letters can keep using your original bitmap font. The blue template is separate from the exported lines.

The grid uses x = 0…5 and y = 0…10, with the origin at the upper left. Each glyph allows 16 independent lines, with up to 12 vertices (11 segments) each. One click and Finish dot creates a dot. Export also finishes the current drawing. Returning to the first cell creates a closed path.
